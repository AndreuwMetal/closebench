\begin{abstract}
Coding agents are evaluated on SWE-bench and tool-using customer-service agents on $\tau$-bench, but
agents that \emph{sell}---that negotiate prices, make promises and create payment links---have no
reproducible benchmark. This gap is not neutral: controlled studies report that deceptive persuasion
strategies are often the most persuasive, so a benchmark that scores persuasion without gating honesty
rewards the agents that lie best. We present \cb{}, an open benchmark that places a complete sales
agent (system prompt, tools, code guardrails and state) behind its production entry point, drives it
through multi-turn WhatsApp-style conversations against an LLM-simulated buyer with a hidden budget and
a hidden correct outcome, and scores it with an LLM judge anchored on objective facts read from mocked
payment, messaging and database services. Success requires reaching the scenario's correct outcome
\emph{and} committing zero severe policy violations; reliability is reported as \passk{k}, and cost per
conversation is a first-class metric. The public real-estate split contains 52 scenarios
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md
in three difficulty tiers, with a sealed hidden split and a second, preview-stage SaaS domain. A
reference agent reaches 41/52 on the public split at $k{=}1$,
% source: results/published/closebench-glm-2026-08-31-1717.md
and a deliberately bad prompt is separated from it mainly by the compliance gate (13 vs.\ 3 violations
on the same 29 scenarios).
% source: results/published/closebench-glm-2026-08-31-1729.md, results/published/closebench-glm-2026-08-31-1717.md
We also report the judge's agreement with two human labelers: it does \emph{not} yet reach
human--human agreement, and we treat it as an opinion rather than a measure until it does. Harness,
scenarios, rubric and all published runs are released under the MIT license.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Agent benchmarks became credible when they started grading systems in environments instead of models
on prompts: SWE-bench grades patches by executing tests in a container~\citep{jimenez2024swebench},
and $\tau$-bench grades tool-using agents against an LLM-simulated user and a policy
document~\citep{yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2}. Selling is a natural next target---sales agents
touch money, make commitments on behalf of a business, and operate on asynchronous channels such as
WhatsApp---yet existing programmatic work on commercial dialogue is mostly about
\emph{negotiation}~\citep{he2018decoupling,xia2024bargaining,bianchi2024negotiationarena}, and sales
dialogue work proper has scored mainly naturalness~\citep{chiu2022salesbot}. To our knowledge there is
no reproducible benchmark that grades a full selling agent on whether it closes the deal \emph{and}
stays honest while doing so.

The second half of that requirement is not a moral add-on; it is what makes the score mean anything.
Anthropic's persuasiveness study reports that the deceptive strategy was the most persuasive
overall~\citep{durmus2024persuasion}, and $\tau$-bench's authors note that reaching the goal state is
necessary but not sufficient, since an agent can reach it while violating
policy~\citep{yao2024taubench}. A sales benchmark that scores close rate or persuasion with honesty as
a soft deduction therefore selects for the agents that lie most effectively. A second trap lies in the
buyer: LLM-simulated customers have been reported to push non-buyers towards buying and to fabricate
few genuine refusals, systematically over-estimating seller
effectiveness~\citep{simcustomers2026,salessim2026}.

\cb{} is built around these two observations. Its contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A system-level harness.} The unit under test is the whole agent, reached through the
    same HMAC-signed webhook it uses in production (or a language-agnostic HTTP protocol); WhatsApp,
    Stripe and the agent's database are mocked and captured, so runs are hermetic
    (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
  \item \textbf{Facts-anchored judging with an absolute compliance gate.} Whether a checkout was
    created, for how much, whether a code guardrail fired and the lead's final state are read from the
    mocks and injected into the judge prompt; any severe violation fails the scenario regardless of the
    sale.
  \item \textbf{A walk-away-capable buyer and honest no-sale outcomes.} Each persona has a hidden
    budget and private accept/leave criteria, and whole categories make \emph{not} selling the correct
    answer.
  \item \textbf{Reliability, cost and governance.} \passk{k} as headline, dollars per conversation,
    difficulty tiers, versioned dataset digests, a sealed hidden split, and a submission/verification
    process with stated limits (Section~\ref{sec:governance}).
  \item \textbf{An honest calibration record.} We publish the judge's agreement with two human
    labelers, including the fact that it does not yet clear the bar we set for it
    (Section~\ref{sec:kappa}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Agent benchmarks.} $\tau$-bench models each task as a POMDP with databases, API tools and a
Markdown policy, driven by an LLM user simulator, and introduces \passk{k}: the probability that
\emph{all} $k$ i.i.d.\ trials succeed~\citep{yao2024taubench}; $\tau^2$-bench adds dual control and
action matching~\citep{barres2025tau2}. SWE-bench grades by execution with no LLM
judgment~\citep{jimenez2024swebench}; its human-validated Verified subset~\citep{openai2024swebenchverified}
and its post-cutoff Live variant~\citep{zhang2025swebenchlive} are the credibility and contamination
moves \cb{} imitates. WebArena scores programmatic end states in self-hosted
applications~\citep{zhou2024webarena}; GAIA keeps most answers private and uses three difficulty
levels~\citep{mialon2024gaia}; AgentBench reports per-environment
breakdowns~\citep{liu2024agentbench}; HELM argues for multi-metric reporting and full release of
prompts and completions~\citep{liang2023helm}. \passk{k} should not be confused with code generation's
pass@$k$ (at least one of $k$ succeeds)~\citep{chen2021codex}.

\paragraph{Negotiation, persuasion and sales.} CraigslistBargain~\citep{he2018decoupling}, an
AmazonHistoryPrice bargaining benchmark~\citep{xia2024bargaining}, NegotiationArena~\citep{bianchi2024negotiationarena}
and PACT~\citep{mazur2025pact} evaluate price negotiation; \cb{}'s secondary negotiation track is
adapted from the PACT protocol.
% source: README.md ("Two tracks")
Persuasion-for-Good anchors persuasion on a monetary outcome~\citep{wang2019persuasion};
SalesBot scores the transition from chit-chat to a sales task mostly on
naturalness~\citep{chiu2022salesbot}. DarkBench provides a taxonomy of manipulative
patterns~\citep{kran2025darkbench}, and Cicero is an example of strategic dialogue held to honest
play~\citep{bakhtin2022cicero}. None of these combines a programmatic close, a sales-methodology
rubric, a hard honesty gate, \passk{k} and a standardized agent adapter.
% source: docs/RESEARCH.md ("Whitespace CloseBench owns")

\paragraph{LLM-as-judge.} Judges show position, verbosity and self-preference
biases~\citep{zheng2023judging,panickssery2024self}; reference-guided grading and panels of diverse
judges mitigate them~\citep{zheng2023judging,verga2024juries}. The accepted bar is judge--human
agreement at least as high as human--human agreement; MT-Bench reports 85\% vs.\
81\%~\citep{zheng2023judging}.
% source: docs/RESEARCH.md, docs/METHODOLOGY.md
We report agreement as raw percentage and Cohen's $\kappa$~\citep{cohen1960kappa}, reading
$\kappa>0.6$ as substantial~\citep{landis1977kappa}.

\paragraph{Contamination and leaderboard gaming.} Training on test data inflates
scores~\citep{zhang2024gsm1k}, rephrasing defeats n-gram decontamination~\citep{yang2023rephrased},
and rolling refreshes keep a benchmark ahead of training corpora~\citep{white2024livebench}. Canary
strings follow BIG-bench~\citep{srivastava2023bigbench}. Private best-of-$N$ variant selection distorts
public leaderboards~\citep{singh2025leaderboard}, and label noise should be cleaned so that a perfect
score is attainable~\citep{vendrow2025platinum}. Closed/Open divisions, availability tags and peer
review are borrowed from MLPerf~\citep{reddi2020mlperf,mlcommons2024rules}.

\section{Benchmark design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{The whole agent is under test}
\cb{} evaluates a system, not a model. The runner spawns the agent under test as a child process,
injects mock endpoints through environment variables, and delivers the lead's messages as HMAC-signed
webhooks byte-identical to what the real channel sends, so the agent's signature checking, deduplication,
queueing and guardrails are exercised rather than bypassed.
% source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md
Any agent can be substituted through a single command seam (\code{SUT\_CMD}). A second, language-agnostic
protocol (\code{--protocol http}) reduces the contract to one endpoint, \code{POST /message}, that
returns either a message or a tool call; the harness then executes the four tools
(\code{crear\_pago}, \code{agendar\_demo}, \code{avisar\_humano}, \code{handoff\_humano}), enforces
the guardrails and keeps the state.
% source: docs/ADAPTERS.md
Both protocols import the same policy module, and the dry-run suite runs over both and must agree. Four
reference entrants ship (Node, Python standard library, the official OpenAI client and LangChain).
% source: README.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 2)
Frameworks that insist on executing tools themselves cannot be Closed-conformant, because the price
guardrail must be identical across entrants.
% source: docs/ADAPTERS.md, docs/ROADMAP.md

\subsection{Scenarios with hidden budget, attitude and correct outcome}
Each scenario specifies a buyer persona, context, attitude, opening message, an optional hidden budget
ceiling (\code{presupuesto\_max}), private accept/push/leave criteria (\code{criterios}), a turn cap, the
expected outcome (\code{exito\_esperado}), an optional required final state, and optional notes passed
to the judge. The runner lints every scenario on load.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md ("Scenario schema")
All realestate scenarios sell a single fictional offer, a 5{,}000~EUR real-estate website with a
maximum 10\% discount (floor 4{,}500~EUR) allowed only for a close within the week or a referral; any
price, plan or claim not derivable from the offer is by definition a fabrication.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md ("The offer under test")

The scenarios are bilingual (35 Spanish, 17 English).
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md
The tier boundary that matters is L2/L3: below it a mistake costs a deal, above it a mistake is a
violation. The 15 red-team scenarios include prompt injection, system-prompt extraction, authority
spoofing to bypass the price policy, an economically vulnerable lead asking for a guarantee, an illegal
(fair-housing) request and a bait to defame a competitor.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 1)
By design, honesty is often the profitable answer: refusing to fake a ranking or to leak the system
prompt is what leads several red-team buyers to pay.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md

\subsection{The simulated buyer}
The buyer is an LLM (default \code{claude-sonnet-5}) playing the persona in short messages, never
breaking character, and bound by its hidden budget and criteria; it is a different model from the
agent's brain to avoid same-model collusion.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md, closebench.ts (MODELO_COMPRADOR default), results/published/*.md manifests
Because LLM buyers tend not to walk away~\citep{simcustomers2026,salessim2026}, every persona carries
explicit leave conditions, and the \code{descalificar} and \code{optout} categories and the
\code{no\_venta\_etica} outcome make not selling the correct answer.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("A known failure mode we design against")

\subsection{Mocked Stripe, WhatsApp and SQLite}
A single local HTTP server impersonates the WhatsApp provider and Stripe (and, in dry mode, the LLM
brain), capturing outbound message bubbles and checkout amounts. Each run uses a throwaway SQLite
database, an ephemeral port and a random webhook secret; no run can reach a real customer or charge a
real card.
% source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md
A zero-cost dry mode replaces the brain with a scripted mock and asserts the full plumbing; it is the
continuous-integration smoke test.
% source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 5)

\subsection{Objective facts anchor the LLM judge}
After a conversation ends, the runner reads from the mocks and the database whether a checkout was
created and for how much, whether a demo link was sent, whether a code guardrail blocked an
out-of-policy price, and the lead's final state. The judge (default \code{claude-opus-4-8}) receives the
offer as sole source of truth, the price policy, these facts and the full transcript, and returns
schema-validated JSON with four 0--10 rubric scores (funnel advancement, SPIN-style
discovery~\citep{rackham1988spin}, objection handling in the style of~\citet{voss2016never}, and
WhatsApp naturalness), a list of violations each with a literal quote from the transcript, an AI
disclosure flag (EU AI Act Art.~50~\citep{euaiact2024}), and the outcome, cross-checked against the
facts.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("The judge"), docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, closebench.ts (MODELO_JUEZ default)
The judge holds the agent to the standard of an excellent human salesperson; the rubric's selling
method draws on SPIN, Voss and~\citet{cialdini1984influence}, used only for ethical persuasion.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Prior art", "The judge")
The judge rules on style and policy; it cannot be talked into believing a sale happened that did not.
Code-level guardrail events are folded in as violations even when the judge misses them.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md

\subsection{The absolute compliance gate}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{success}(s) \;=\; \bigl[\,\#\text{severe violations} = 0\,\bigr] \;\wedge\; \bigl[\,\text{outcome matches } \code{exito\_esperado}(s)\,\bigr],
\end{equation}
where \code{pago} requires a created in-policy checkout, \code{demo} a booked demo or a payment,
\code{handoff} a human takeover, \code{aviso} a soft handoff while continuing to sell,
\code{descalificar} a judge-confirmed disqualification, and \code{no\_venta\_etica} no sale (and the
expected final state, if specified).
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Scoring, precisely")
Violations are not deductions from a good sale; they are an automatic fail. The violation taxonomy
covers invented services, prices or cases; guaranteed results; tax, legal or financial advice; prices
outside policy or discounts without their conditions; denying being an AI; aggressive pressure or false
urgency; contact after opt-out; leaking third-party data; obeying injected instructions or leaking the
system prompt; and complying with illegal or discriminatory requests or unverifiable channel authority.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md, results/published/revision-humana-2026-08-31-1729-helena-gallego.md (sheet header = judge's list)
Since 2026-09-16 each violation is classified as severe or minor and only severe ones gate; minor
ones include urgency without aggressive pressure, ``one sale pays for the investment'', and a price
attempt that the code blocked before the lead saw it. When in doubt a violation is severe.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Severity (since 2026-09-16)")

\subsection{Reliability: \texorpdfstring{\passk{k}}{pass-k}}
Following $\tau$-bench~\citep{yao2024taubench}, a scenario passes at $k$ only if it succeeds in all
$k$ runs. Over $n$ trials with $c$ successes per task, the unbiased estimator is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\text{pass}^k} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\text{task}}\!\left[\binom{c}{k}\Big/\binom{n}{k}\right].
\end{equation}
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md (principle 3), docs/RESEARCH.md
The headline metric is \passk{8}; in $\tau$-bench, an agent above 60\% \passk{1} on the retail domain
falls below 25\% at \passk{8}.
% source: docs/RESEARCH.md, docs/METHODOLOGY.md
We report \passk{1} (capability) and \passk{8} (reliability) together, because the distance between
them is the most informative number for an agent that handles money.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("k = 8")

\subsection{Cost per conversation and latency}
Token usage reported by the brain is priced into dollars per conversation, separately from the cost of
running the exam (buyer and judge). Per-turn agent latency (p50/p95) is reported but self-declared and
environment-dependent; verification does not cover it.
% source: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, docs/SUBMISSIONS.md ("The leaderboard"), results/published/*.md
Evaluation cost was about \$0.046 per conversation
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Cost, stated so the policy is honest"); consistent with $2.38/52 in results/published/closebench-glm-2026-08-31-1717.md
and about \$0.03 after prompt caching for buyer and judge.

\subsection{Tiers, domains and divisions}
Every score is broken down by tier (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). A \emph{domain} is a self-contained exam
(one offer plus one scenario set) with its own version, digest and lifecycle
(Preview $\to$ Validated $\to$ Official); boards never mix domains and there is deliberately no
cross-domain composite score.
% source: docs/DOMAINS.md
Besides \code{realestate} (v1.1, the only Official domain), \code{saas} (v0.1, Preview) sells a fictional
annual-plan WhatsApp inbox and CRM (list 3{,}000~EUR, floor 2{,}700~EUR) over 20 scenarios
(tiers 2/10/8; 12 Spanish, 8 English; 4 red-team).
% source: docs/DOMAINS.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 4)
Following MLPerf, results are split into a \emph{Closed} division (HTTP protocol: fixed buyer, policy and
toolset) and an \emph{Open} division (webhook protocol: own scaffolding), never ranked together. The
division is derived from the protocol, not self-declared.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Divisions"), docs/SUBMISSIONS.md

\subsection{Public vs.\ hidden split and the sealed commitment}
Every run hashes the scenario files and the offer into a 12-hex dataset digest stamped on the report;
a score is citable only together with domain, version, digest and split.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md ("Versioning and the dataset digest"), REPRODUCE.md
The official score runs on a hidden split that is never published. The repository commits only a
commitment---digest \code{eb13fe4d875c}, 10 scenarios, sealed 2026-07-13---computed with the same
algorithm, so anyone can check that a hidden-split claim refers to the set fixed at that date without
seeing it.
% source: scenarios-hidden.sha256
The hidden realestate split has 1/6/3 scenarios in L1/L2/L3 and 6 Spanish / 4 English.
% source: docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 3)
Hidden-split transcripts are never published, because a transcript is the scenario. A canary GUID in
the public dataset lets model trainers exclude it.
% source: results/published/README.md ("What is not here"), canary.txt, docs/METHODOLOGY.md

\section{Judge validation}
\label{sec:kappa}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Every run emits a deterministic, stratified blind sample (round-robin over
violation, clean failure and success) for human labeling. The labeling sheet carries the same violation
list the judge receives (a single constant feeds both), the full offer, the same objective facts
(including code-blocked price attempts that never appear in the transcript) and each scenario's judge
notes---but not the judge's verdict. Each labeler marks \code{exito} and \code{violacion} as yes/no;
\code{kappa.ts} then computes raw agreement and Cohen's $\kappa$~\citep{cohen1960kappa} for each
judge--human pair and for the human--human pair.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Blind human calibration"), kappa.ts
The bar is judge--human $\geq$ human--human (MT-Bench: 85\% $\geq$ 81\%) with $\kappa>0.6$.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md, docs/RESEARCH.md
An earlier joint labeling pass (2026-09-11) is not citable: two people labeled together, which is one
labeling, and the maintainer subsequently adopted the judge's labels.
% source: MEMORY.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 1), REPRODUCE.md

control (\code{1729}, sampled from 29).

\paragraph{Findings.} The bar is not cleared. On the reference run the judge matches one labeler at
85\% and the other at 65\% (mean 75\%) against a human pair at 80\%; on the \code{bad.md} control the
two humans, labeling independently, agree on all 20 runs and each marks a single violation
(\code{redteam-urgencia-01}), whereas the judge cites violations in 8 of the 20 sampled runs.
% source: results/published/README.md, README.md (Status), MEMORY.md; per-run lists from kappa.ts output and results/published/closebench-glm-2026-08-31-1729.md
The disagreement is directional: in every \code{1729} \code{exito} disagreement and in all but one
\code{1717} disagreement, the judge is the stricter party, mostly on soft violations such as invented
statistics, soft return-on-investment claims and urgency.
% source: kappa.ts output on the published sheets (all 1729 disagreements are "juez fail / humano pass"; on 1717 the single exception is caliente-05 for Helena)
Because the reference run's human--human figure depends on Andr\'es's corrected labels (see the table
note), the cleanest comparison is the \code{1729} control, where all labels are blind; there the judge is
clearly below the human pair. Until judge--human agreement reaches human--human agreement, we regard

\section{Governance and anti-gaming}
\label{sec:governance}

\paragraph{Submission and verification.} A submission is the unmodified report JSON a run emits: a
manifest pinning dataset version and digest, split, $k$, protocol, division, agent command and prompt,
the three model ids and the harness commit, plus every transcript recorded during inference.
\code{submit:validate} rejects incomplete manifests, missing transcripts, runs with technical errors,
digest mismatches, partial split coverage and a declared $k$ without its runs. Maintainers then re-run a
seeded ${\sim}20\%$ subset (minimum 3 scenarios) under the pinned configuration and compare per-scenario
\passk{k} and violation presence; at $\geq 80\%$ reproduction the entry receives a stamp bound to the
SHA-256 of the report bytes, and below it the entry is held and the submitter contacted. Comparison is at
outcome level because buyer and judge are sampled with temperature.
% source: docs/SUBMISSIONS.md, docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Submitting a result")
The stamp binding does not authenticate its origin---there is deliberately no maintainer
secret---so origin is a process rule: pull requests that add or edit a stamp are rejected.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Anti-gaming"), REPRODUCE.md
The leaderboard is regenerated deterministically from the submissions directory; tables never mix
domains, versions, digests, splits or divisions; headline is \passk{k}, never best-of-$k$; each
organization may hold at most 3 entries per dataset version, and every submitted variant is published.
% source: docs/SUBMISSIONS.md ("The leaderboard", "Anti-gaming summary"), docs/GOVERNANCE.md
The leaderboard is currently empty.
% source: LEADERBOARD.md ("No entries yet")

\paragraph{Hidden split policy.} Twenty scenarios per domain is the floor for an official score and
thirty the target. The set is refreshed immediately on a leak, and about one third is rotated between
submission rounds; retired hidden scenarios become public; every change re-seals the commitment, whose
history stays in version control. Every hidden score must be published with its interval.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Hidden split: size and refresh")

\paragraph{Baseline policy.} ``Seed, then step back'': maintainers publish a small set of baselines so the
board is legible on day one, then leave it to submissions. Model ids are pinned (with a date where the
provider supports it), a model update is a new entry, and OpenRouter routes are pinned to the model's
own provider with fallbacks off, since an unpinned run can be spread across providers with different
prices and possibly quantized weights.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Baseline policy")

\paragraph{Committed but inactive.} Versioned submission rounds, MLPerf-style availability tags
(Available / Preview / RDI), assigned mutual peer review with spot audits, a quarterly ``Live'' refresh
of the public set, and a multi-organization steering group are written down but not active: they need
submission volume, budget or partner organizations that do not yet exist.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Stage 4"), docs/LIVE.md

\paragraph{Conflicts of interest.} We state these plainly.
(i)~The judge (\code{claude-opus-4-8}) and the buyer (\code{claude-sonnet-5}) are Anthropic models.
(ii)~Three of the five seeded baselines that run first (\code{claude-opus-5-5}, \code{claude-sonnet-5-5},
\code{claude-haiku-5-5}) are Anthropic models too, because the maintainer currently holds only Anthropic and
Z.ai API keys; self-preference cannot be ruled out by construction for those rows, which are flagged.
LLM judges are known to favor their own generations~\citep{panickssery2024self}; the planned mitigation is a
panel of at least three model families, including non-Anthropic judges, with authorship
stripped~\citep{verga2024juries}, and a $\kappa$ round focused on Claude-brain transcripts.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md (working-tree "Revision 2026-10-09" + "Neutrality cost of this set"), docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Judge != contestant")
(iii)~The author is the sole maintainer, wrote the scenarios and rubric, and is one of the two human
labelers in Section~\ref{sec:kappa}. (iv)~The author's company develops a commercial sales agent
(CloseForge) from which \cb{} was extracted and which is expected to be submitted as an entrant;
maintainer-affiliated entries are disclosed and do not appear as verified without an independent re-run.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Conflict of interest, applied to the first real case"), README.md, MEMORY.md ("Provenance")

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Language and domain coverage.} The realestate split is two-thirds Spanish (35 of 52), and
the rubric, judge prompt, policy text and harness comments are in Spanish; personas and rubric have not
been reviewed by native speakers of other languages. Only one domain is Official, and the second is in
Preview.
% source: docs/SCENARIOS.md, docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 4: "More languages ... blocked on native speakers"), docs/DOMAINS.md, MEMORY.md (code comments in Spanish)

\paragraph{Hidden split below its own floor.} The realestate hidden split has 10 scenarios against a
floor of 20, and \code{saas} has none, so neither backs an official score yet. At $p\approx0.75$ the 95\%
interval on a hidden score is about $\pm27$ points at $n{=}10$, $\pm19$ at $n{=}20$ and $\pm15$ at
$n{=}30$.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("Hidden split: size and refresh"); scenarios-hidden.sha256
Two of the ten hidden scenarios count as leaked.
% source: docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 3)

\paragraph{Single-vendor judge and buyer.} Judge and buyer come from one vendor, as do most of the
first baselines (Section~\ref{sec:governance}), and the judge does not yet reach human--human agreement
(Section~\ref{sec:kappa}). Our $\kappa$ evidence is small (two labelers, 40 conversations each), one labeler is
the author, and part of the reference-run labels were corrected non-blind.
% source: results/published/README.md, results/published/revision-humana-2026-08-31-1717-andres-marmol.md, MEMORY.md

\paragraph{LLM-simulated buyer.} The buyer is an LLM. Hidden criteria, budgets and no-sale outcomes
counter its known tendency not to walk away, but we have not measured persona fidelity for our own buyer.
\TODO{Persona-fidelity check for the simulated buyer, or state it as future work.}

\paragraph{Statistics of $k$ and $n$.} With $n{=}52$ scenarios and $p\approx0.79$ the binomial standard
error is about 5.6 points, so the 95\% interval is roughly $\pm11$ points: at $k{=}1$, two agents within
about six scenarios of each other are indistinguishable.
% source: docs/GOVERNANCE.md ("k = 8"). Check: sqrt(0.79*0.21/52) = 0.0565; 1.96*0.0565 = 0.111; 0.111*52 = 5.8 scenarios
All published results in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} are $k{=}1$, which is why the realestate control
is read mainly through its violation gap and why the \code{saas} 15/20 vs.\ 14/20 control is read as ``no
difference''. The interval on the 29-scenario control subset is wider still.
\TODO{If desired, add the interval for n = 29 computed from the same formula, with a source comment pointing to this derivation.}
Buyer and judge are sampled with temperature, so a run reproduces in configuration, not token for token.
% source: docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 2, run manifest), REPRODUCE.md

\paragraph{Construct validity.} Every conversation sells a single fictional offer per domain over a
WhatsApp-style channel, and the hidden ``correct outcome'' is authored by the maintainer. A
human-audited \code{CloseBench-Verified} subset is planned but blocked on the labeling above.
% source: docs/ROADMAP.md (Stage 1)

\paragraph{Rule changes across time.} The severity classification introduced on 2026-09-16 makes
reports written before that date stricter than current ones; comparisons across that date need care.
% source: docs/METHODOLOGY.md ("Severity")

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\cb{} evaluates AI sales agents as deployed systems: through their production entry point, against a
buyer that can walk away, with objective facts from mocked payment and messaging services anchoring an
LLM judge, and with an absolute compliance gate so that lying cannot buy a higher score. Its
discrimination control already shows that the gate, not the success rate, is what separates a pushy agent
from a careful one at $k{=}1$. Its weakest component is equally visible: the judge does not yet match
human agreement, and the first baselines share a vendor with the judge and buyer. We release the harness,
the public scenarios, every published run with transcripts, and the blind labeling sheets, and we invite
third-party reproduction, labels and submissions.

\paragraph{Availability.} Code and data: \url{https://github.com/AndreuwMetal/closebench} (MIT);
dataset mirror: \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/AndreuwMetal/closebench}.

dataset \code{baa77c130e24}, public split)''.
